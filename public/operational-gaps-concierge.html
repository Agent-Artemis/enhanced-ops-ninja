The 5 Operational Gaps quietly costing you.

The gaps that show up again and again in concierge and direct-primary-care practices growing past their first panel — and what it looks like when each one is closed.

I spent years running healthcare operations before I married that experience to technology. Since then I've talked with a lot of concierge and DPC physicians who left insurance medicine to build something better — and who are now growing faster than their back office. The same five gaps come up almost every time. Here they are, honestly, along with what it looks like when they're fixed.

01

Your EMR is built for insurance, not membership

Symptom

Your software was designed around claims, coding, and payers — but you run on relationships and subscriptions. So you fight the system every day to make it fit the practice you actually built.

What it costs

Clicks and workarounds where you wanted simplicity. The tech works against your model instead of for it — and every new patient inherits the friction.

Closed

Tooling built around membership medicine — the patient relationship at the center, not the claim.

02

Membership revenue leaking quietly

Symptom

Failed cards no one chased, memberships that expired without a renewal, patients who quietly lapsed months ago and you only noticed at year-end.

What it costs

Recurring revenue is your entire model — so a few points of churn you can't see is a real dent in the year, compounding every month.

Closed

Billing, renewals, and failed payments tracked automatically, with lapses flagged before they cost you the member.

03

Onboarding done by hand

Symptom

New-member intake, welcome, records transfer, first-visit scheduling — pieced together manually, and a little differently every time.

What it costs

Your best chance at a first impression is your most manual moment — slow, inconsistent, and easy to drop right when the member is deciding if you were worth it.

Closed

A smooth, repeatable onboarding that runs itself — so every member's first two weeks feel as premium as the promise you sold.

04

The physician is the operations layer

Symptom

You're answering billing questions, fixing the schedule, chasing paperwork — the ops fall to the one person who should be practicing medicine.

What it costs

Your clinical time — the thing members actually pay for — gets eaten by admin, and you can't add a provider without multiplying the mess.

Closed

Operations that run without you in the middle, so you can grow the panel or add physicians without growing the chaos.

05

Flying blind on the economics

Symptom

Panel size, revenue per member, retention, capacity — the numbers that actually run a membership practice live in your head or a spreadsheet you update once a quarter.

What it costs

You're making growth decisions on instinct instead of instruments — when to raise the fee, when to hire, when you're truly at capacity — all a guess.

Closed

The metrics that run the practice on a live dashboard — so you know exactly when to raise, hire, or expand.
