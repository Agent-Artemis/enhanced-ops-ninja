The seven you will hear most

I already have a dashboard.

Does everyone in the room trust the same number? Most dashboards get quietly checked against somebody's own spreadsheet before anyone acts on them. A dashboard nobody argues with is a different product from a dashboard nobody believes.

My controller pulls the numbers.

She does, and she is good at it — that is the problem. How many days a month is she spending on it, and what would you rather she did with them? A person doing this well is the most expensive way to do it, and it stops when she is away.

My data is a mess.

That is the job, not a prerequisite. You do not tidy the house before the cleaner arrives. Nobody comes in and asks you to clean it up first — sorting it out is the first two weeks of the work.

We are too small for this.

If you run more than one location and more than two systems that do not talk to each other, you are exactly the size. It is not about revenue — it is about how many places a number can hide.

How long does it take?

Two to three weeks to the first live number. Not a discovery phase, not a pilot — a number on a screen you can use.

What if we stop?

Six months to start, then month to month. Your data is yours and never leaves your own accounts — we join as an invited guest and you can remove us. The system that reads it is ours and we run it, so if it ends we switch it off: you keep every system and every number you had before, and what you lose is the screens.

It is expensive.

Against what? Turn it into a comparison, never a defence. Depending on how many systems and locations we connect it runs $4,500 to $9,000 a month, which is the cost of a low to mid-level employee. If you needed this information and someone had to pull it together by hand, would you hesitate to hire someone to complete that work? Think of this as a digital employee. It does not take PTO, does not need managing, does not leave after eighteen months, and it reads six systems before the 7am huddle. Then ask what the last decision made on a wrong number cost. They will have one.

Commercial & field services

ServiceTitan already gives me dashboards.

ServiceTitan reports on ServiceTitan. It does not know what QuickBooks says. The gap between them is where your margin hides — the completed job it counts and the invoice that never got raised are the same work, in two systems, with two different answers.

My techs' numbers are in the field, not in a system.

Then that is the first thing worth fixing, and it is days of work rather than quarters. If drive time is not recorded you cannot tell a capacity problem from a routing one — and those have completely different answers.

We are only one branch.

Then it is truck by truck instead of branch by branch. Same question, smaller unit. One branch with five trucks has the same problem as five branches — it is just closer to home.

Distribution & wholesale

The ERP is our system of record.

For accounting, yes. It is not the system of record for whether the truck left on time — and the carrier portal does not tell it. The gap between them is where your service level actually lives.

We count stock twice a year.

So twice a year you know. The other three hundred and sixty-three days you are guessing, and every purchasing decision in between is made on the guess.

Our fill rate is fine.

Measured how? Line fill, order fill and case fill give three different answers off the same week — and the flattering one is usually the one being quoted. It is worth knowing which one you have been reporting.

Manufacturing

The MES already shows OEE.

On the machines it is connected to, on its own definition of availability. It does not know what the ERP costed the run at. Two systems, two OEEs, and neither of them is wrong — which is exactly the problem.

Our operators log all of this.

On paper, at the end of the shift, from memory. That is a record, not a measurement — and nobody is going to act on Tuesday's downtime when they read it on Friday.

We are not that automated.

Then the shop-floor data is the gap, and closing it is a much smaller job than an MES rollout. You do not need a connected factory to know which cell is losing you money.

Restaurants & food service

Restaurant365 already does this.

R365 is only as current as what has been entered into it, and it does not know what Toast rang up an hour ago. The decision you make on a Wednesday happens before R365 has the data to inform it.

My managers know their numbers.

Each on their own definition. Ask two of them how they calculate labour percentage — whether salaried managers are in it, whether it is against gross or net sales. You will get two answers, and both will be sincere.

We are seasonal, it swings around.

Then comparing your sites to each other matters more, not less. Seasonality hits all of them; the gap between them is the part you control.

Professional services

Practice management already reports utilization.

On recorded time. It does not know what was actually collected, and the difference between those two is the only number that matters. Utilization can be up and the firm can be poorer.

Our people are fully booked.

Booked, billable and collected are three different numbers. Being busy is not the same as being paid, and a fully booked team writing off fifteen per cent at billing is the most expensive kind of busy.

Every matter is different.

Which is exactly why an average hides it. The point is seeing which ones differ, while you can still do something about them.

PE portfolio companies

We get monthly reporting from each company.

In each company's own format, on each company's own definitions. The consolidation is a person with a spreadsheet — and that person is the system. Ask what happens the month they are away.

Our companies use different systems and always will.

That is the assumption this is built on. Nothing gets replaced. The systems stay where they are and the definitions get reconciled on top of them.

That is what we have a CFO for.

You do — and this is what he is spending three days a month on. The question is not whether he can do it. It is whether that is the highest-value thing he does all month.
