What this room can sell

Everything ranked below runs on equipment already installed. No capex is assumed. Two facts decide the order: the homogenizer is far smaller than the bottling line, and the regulation does not get lighter when the physical job gets simpler.

Act on this one regardless of everything else. FDA food facility registration must be renewed in the window that is open now, 1 October to 31 December 2026, every even year, with no grace period. If it lapses, food from the facility is deemed adulterated under 21 CFR 1.241. Supplements count as food for this purpose. Ask on Monday whether the 2026 renewal has been filed, and if it has not, that is the most urgent thing in the building.

The homogenizer runs 50 litres a day. One ounce is 29.57 mL, so 50 litres is 1,690 bottles a day on a single pass, or about 1,650 if you round an ounce to a flat 30 mL, which is where the figure in the brief comes from. The bottling line runs 800 an hour, which is 6,400 in an eight hour shift. The filler is roughly 3.8 times larger than the thing that feeds it, leaving about 4,700 bottles a day, near 94,000 a month, of filling capacity the homogenizer cannot supply.

One number in the 23 September deal notes needs correcting before it is used again. It reads "capacity 10,000 bottles/day = $250K/day". That 10,000 is the bottling line, not the product. On a single pass the room can make 1,690 bottles a day, about $42,000 at $25. The claim overstates the ceiling by roughly six times, and it is in a document Blake has read.

Three facts that set the order of the list

1. Filling is not the regulation light option. 21 CFR 111.1 applies supplement cGMP to anyone who will "manufacture, package, label, or hold a dietary supplement", including one "you manufacture but that is packaged or labeled by another person" (eCFR). Filling and capping somebody else's supplement puts BioVara inside Part 111 exactly as if they had formulated it. The simplest physical job carries the same gate as the hardest one and earns the least per bottle.

2. ⭐ So within contract work, skincare is lighter than supplements. MoCRA requires facility registration and product listing, but the cosmetic GMP rule was never finalised: the proposed rule was due 29 December 2024 and the final 29 December 2025, neither happened, and FDA moved it to long term actions. A cosmetic fill job today carries registration and listing. An identical supplement fill job drags all of Part 111. Same line, same idle capacity, different gate.

3. Setup is about $2,000 a run (MSL). Spread across the run that is $4.00 a bottle at 500 units, $0.40 at 5,000, $0.10 at 20,000. A fill fee is $0.75 to $1.50 a unit, so a 500 unit job is underwater on setup alone. Lead with the offers that repeat, not the ones with a low minimum. Small jobs are where the margin dies.

And the one that is easy to miss: adding more BioVara liposomal SKUs does not add capacity, it splits it. Glutathione, vitamin C, B12, CoQ10, curcumin and berberine all run through the same homogenizer as NAD. On a constrained machine a second SKU is a substitution decision, not a growth decision. Only work that avoids the homogenizer is genuinely additive.

What I would not put in the first wave

The 2oz Hotworx shot. It needs a shot or stick pack filler, which is equipment, and at 2oz each unit draws twice the homogenizer volume, halving the ceiling to about 845 a day. The channel is real and growing, which is why it deserves a capacity answer rather than a quick yes.

Pet liposomal. Same scarce machine time as a human SKU, a regulator nobody in the room has dealt with, and no customer currently asking.

Anything that needs a purchase, because there is no equipment budget.

Peptides: the brief was wrong and this is the corrected position

A contract manufacturer cannot "route via 503(a)". 503(a) compounding is an activity of a licensed pharmacy against a patient specific prescription. It is not a route a manufacturer can take.

BPC-157. Correction to the brief, verified on FDA's own page, content current 22 April 2026: it is not on the active Category 2 list, it sits under bulk drug substances nominated but withdrawn (FDA). Removal from Category 2 is not permission. It remains an unapproved drug and not a lawful dietary ingredient, and FDA issued warning letters in 2023 to firms selling it as a supplement.

KPV. Not FDA approved for any human use and not a lawful dietary ingredient. An advisory committee voted in July 2026 to recommend adding it to the 503A list, which is a recommendation and not a final rule.

The three real options are to leave both alone, to become a 503(b) outsourcing facility which means full drug cGMP under 21 CFR 210 and 211 and is a different company, or to supply a pharmacy as an ingredient vendor which carries drug establishment registration of its own.

Recommendation: peptides come off the first meeting entirely, except GHK-Cu as a cosmetic.

Questions to ask them

In order. The first three can delete whole revenue lines before anything is sized.

  1. Has the 2026 FDA food facility registration renewal been filed? The window closes 31 December and there is no grace period. If it has lapsed, product from the facility is deemed adulterated.
  2. Are you operating to 21 CFR 111? Specifically: master manufacturing records, batch records, a named quality control unit, identity testing on incoming components, finished product specifications. This gates every supplement line including plain filling.
  3. How many homogenizer passes does NAD need? One pass is 1,690 bottles a day. Three is 564. Nothing can be sized until this is answered.
  4. Are you registered as a cosmetic facility under MoCRA, and have product listings been filed? This gates the skincare work, which is otherwise the lightest contract offer.
  5. Has FDA ever inspected the facility, and were there any Form 483 observations or warning letters?
  6. Any third party certification such as NSF, UL or NPA, or customer audits passed?
  7. Is the clean room classified to an ISO level and certified, or clean but unclassified?
  8. Is the water system USP purified water, and how is it monitored?
  9. Is the room dedicated or shared, and what is the allergen and cross contamination control?
  10. What is the real changeover and clean time between two different products? That number decides whether contract filling is profitable and nobody has measured it.
  11. Is the 0.2 micron sterile filtration validated, and what does it actually run at? A filter slower than the homogenizer would move the constraint.
  12. What is the current shelf life claim and the data behind it? Six months today, twelve as the target, and the export customers depend on it.
  13. What is the actual output today, in bottles a week, against the ceiling above?
  14. On a fill only job, who is the Responsible Person on a cosmetic label, and who holds the supplement label claims? In writing, before the first run.
  15. Who owns the formula IP, and can the room toll process another company's formula without conflict? The existing question with Chris makes this live.
  16. What does batch release look like? Batch records, retained samples, certificate of analysis per lot.
  17. Product liability insurance: what limits, and does it cover contract manufacturing for other brands? Many policies do not.
  18. Do you hold state manufacturer or wholesaler licences?
  19. For a customer formula, who supplies the safety substantiation file and the certificates of analysis for incoming materials?
  20. Do the hemp SKUs being phased out leave any cleaning, segregation or testing obligation on shared equipment after 11 November and 11 December?
  21. What are lead times and minimums on bottles, caps and labels? Contract packing lives or dies on component supply.
  22. If a line needs one piece of equipment, who funds it?

What would make this page true and still wrong

The trap is assuming the simplest physical job is the simplest regulatory job. Plain bottling needs the same registration and the same Part 111 posture as manufacturing, and it earns the least. If BioVara is not registered and not running to Part 111 today, then every supplement line here, including the easiest one, sits behind the same piece of work. That is not a reason to avoid it. It is the reason it is question one.

The second trap is sizing against the wrong machine. Every number here is sized against the homogenizer. Any plan built on the 10,000 a day bottling figure puts a number in front of Tanner that his own room cannot hit.
