What this room can sell

Everything below runs on the equipment already in the room. No capex is assumed and nothing is ranked on equipment we would have to buy. The room is really two plants of very different sizes, and almost every decision follows from that one fact.

The constraint, before anything else

The homogenizer runs 50 litres a day. One ounce is 29.57 mL, so 50 litres is 1,690 bottles a day on a single pass. The bottling line runs 800 an hour, which is 6,400 in an eight hour shift. The filler is roughly 3.8 times larger than the thing that feeds it.

That gap is about 4,700 bottles a day, near 94,000 a month, of filling capacity that the homogenizer cannot supply. It is the most valuable idle asset in the building and it is the reason contract packing appears at the top of the list below.

One number in the 23 September deal notes needs correcting before it is used again. It reads "capacity 10,000 bottles/day = $250K/day". That 10,000 is the bottling line, not the product. On a single pass the room can make 1,690 bottles a day, about $42,000 at $25. The claim overstates the ceiling by roughly six times, and it is in a document Blake has read.

The thing that is easy to miss

Adding more of BioVara's own liposomal products does not add capacity. It splits it. Glutathione, vitamin C, B12, CoQ10, curcumin and berberine all run through the same homogenizer as NAD. Every hour given to a second product is an hour taken from the first. On a constrained machine a second SKU is a substitution decision, not a growth decision.

Work that avoids the homogenizer is the only work that is genuinely additive, which is why filling and packing other people's liquid is ranked first and liposomal toll processing, which is more glamorous, is ranked below it.

Questions to ask them

In order. The first two decide whether anything else on this page is sizeable.

  1. Is the facility FDA registered, and is it cGMP compliant under 21 CFR 111? And separately, is it registered under MoCRA for cosmetics? These are different regimes and the answer may be yes to one and no to the other. Everything we can sell to a third party depends on it.
  2. How many homogenizer passes does NAD need? One pass is 1,690 bottles a day. Three passes is 564. Nothing else on this page can be sized until this is answered.
  3. What is the room's ISO class, and is there environmental monitoring? It decides what we can claim and who we can fill for.
  4. Is the 0.2 micron sterile filtration validated, and what does it actually run at? A filter slower than the homogenizer would move the constraint.
  5. What is the real changeover and clean time between two different products? This is the number that decides whether contract filling is profitable, and nobody has measured it.
  6. What is the water system? Purified water is usually the gate for supplement manufacture.
  7. What is the current shelf life claim and what data sits behind it? Six months today, twelve as the target, and the export customers depend on it.
  8. What is the actual output today, in bottles a week, against the capacity above?
  9. Who owns the formula IP, and can the room toll process someone else's formula without conflict? The existing IP question with Chris makes this a live issue rather than a formality.
  10. What does batch release look like? Batch records, retained samples, certificate of analysis per lot. A contract customer will ask on day one.
  11. What product liability insurance is in place, and does it cover work done for other brands?
  12. What are the lead times and minimums on bottles, caps and labels? Contract packing lives or dies on component supply.
  13. Do the phasing out hemp SKUs leave any cleaning, segregation or testing obligation on shared equipment after 11 November and 11 December?
  14. If a line needs one piece of equipment, who funds it? There is no budget today, so the answer shapes the later section above.
