The Discovery Call

Ten questions for a $10–50M operator. Don't ask all ten. Three, four and seven usually give you the whole picture — a call where you asked six and listened beats one where you asked ten.

Opening — find the pain

1

When you want to know how the business did last month, how do you find out?

Listen for how many systems and how many people get named. That count is your scope.

2

How long does that take, and who does it?

You're pricing their current solution. Usually a controller, three days, every month.

3

By the time you see those numbers, can you still do anything about them?

This is the close, and it lands early. Ask it, then stop talking. Every owner has felt this exact frustration and nobody has ever named it back to them.

Middle — size the mess

4

What systems are you running? Walk me through them.

Accounting, field service, payroll, CRM. You're scoping the build and they don't realise it.

5

Which of those talk to each other today?

Almost always "none." Sometimes a half-built automation nobody trusts.

6

Where does it still come down to a spreadsheet?

The spreadsheet is where the real work hides. Follow up: who owns it, and what happens when they're on holiday?

7

What number do you most wish you could see every morning that you can't?

This is your first deliverable. Build that one thing first and you've won.

Qualify — can they actually buy

8

Has anyone here ever tried to fix this? What happened?

Failed attempts tell you the real obstacle, and whether you're the second vendor in.

9

If you decided this was worth doing, is it your call or does someone else weigh in?

The whole reason we target non-healthcare. If it isn't one person, the cycle just doubled.

Close — surface the trigger

10

Where do you want this business in three years — bigger, sold, or handed to someone?

If they say sold, everything changes. Clean numbers lift the multiple, fragmented systems compress it. You're no longer selling reporting — you're selling the value of the exit.

After the call

Write down their exact words on 6 and 7. The language a buyer uses about their own pain is worth more than anything we would write — that's the copy for the website and the next pitch.

The urgency line, if you need one: "If your competitor gets this first, they see the trends before you do and price against you. This is defence before it's offence."
