4 · How we sell

The opening is clinical, not merchandising

The title we want mostly does not exist here. These are clinical and marketing organisations, not retailers, and only Forum Health publishes a partnerships route. So the first yes comes from a Chief Science Officer or a Chief Medical Officer, and the opening leads with formulation and data rather than with price and minimum order.

The lab is the differentiator

Every other white-label supplier in this category is a contract manufacturer competing on cost. We have an independent PhD-run lab, ex-Merck, running work on the formulation itself. That is third-party lab validation, and study data is available.

  • Stability. Liposomal degradation at the 135 to 150°F a delivery truck reaches in summer. Every chain shipping supplements has this problem and most have not measured it.
  • Bioavailability. Oral against sublingual, subcutaneous and IV. This is the exact objection an IV clinic raises, answered with data rather than assertion.
  • Intracellular NAD assay. No market equivalent.

The pitch is "oral, take-home, your label", not "NAD"

The best-qualified chains already have an NAD ingredient partner. Restore carries Niagen, The DRIPBaR carries NR. The NAD conversation is occupied and arguing it means displacing an incumbent. The oral take-home format is not occupied, and it is additive to what they already sell rather than a replacement for it.

Voice AI at $2,000 a month

Top-of-funnel scheduling. It books discovery calls into this target list so that selling time is spent on calls rather than on dialling. It does not sell and it does not qualify; it fills the calendar.

The retail shelf is not empty, and the incumbent is the same one we meet on the human side. Every one of those is retail and direct-to-consumer, sold through Amazon and the brands' own storefronts. The corporate veterinary clinic shelf is a different channel, stocked through veterinary distributors and vet-exclusive brands. Pet NAD exists at retail. We have not seen it win the veterinary clinic shelf. That second sentence is our reading of what we have found so far, not an established fact.

The structural point: who ships, and who buys

US veterinary distribution runs through three national distributors, Covetrus, MWI Animal Health and Patterson Veterinary, and it is about to be two. Cencora is selling MWI for $1.25 billion in cash plus a 34.3% stake in the combined company. The channel is consolidating from three players to two.

Distributors ship. Like Fullscript on the human side they replace our fulfilment rather than buy from us, which makes them a route to independent clinics rather than a white-label customer. The corporate groups are the operators with own-brand shelves, and those are the genuine white-label buyers. That distinction is the strategy for this channel.

The product: a curcumin chew for joint health

1 · Curcumin is the challenger here, not the incumbent

The vet-recommended leaders are built on glucosamine, chondroitin, ASU, green-lipped mussel and eggshell membrane. Nutramax (Cosequin, Dasuquin) describes itself as the number one vet-recommended retail joint health brand, 25+ years old, with ASU as its differentiator, and it is the competitor that matters. Virbac Movoflex positions explicitly as an alternative to glucosamine and chondroitin, on BiovaFlex eggshell membrane. YuMOVE is green-lipped mussel. Curcumin is not the established answer in this channel, which is the opening and also the work.

2 · The absorption problem is real and is not considered solved

Oral curcumin has poor systemic bioavailability, from rapid intestinal and hepatic metabolism. The literature notes that improvement from enhancers, piperine included, has not reached a satisfactory therapeutic level. This is an open problem, not a solved one.

3 · Competitors are already using serious delivery technology

This is not a category of naive formulations, and it should not be described as one. Several pet curcumin products already carry patented bioavailability technology: Pet Parents SoftSupps (BioPerine and CurQmin), CurcuMagic (Arjuna BCM-95 / Curcugreen), Dr Jones' (BCM-95 and BioPerine), Coco and Luna (BioPerine), PawEver (curcumin phytosome).

What we have not found is a liposomal curcumin pet product. The enhancers in use are piperine, phytosome and patented complexes. That is an absence in our search rather than proof of absence, and it is worth verifying before it is said in a room. Note also that the strongest published evidence in curcumin delivery is phytosome rather than liposomal, curcumin phytosome is reported at 29× the plasma curcuminoid levels of standard curcumin, so the argument is that liposomal is unexplored here, not that it is superior.

4 · There is dog-specific support

A peer-reviewed review reports that in beagle dogs, curcumin beta-cyclodextrin complex, solid dispersion and curcumin phospholipid complex improved bioavailability by over 200%. Phospholipid complex is adjacent to our manufacturer's capability. Canine efficacy work exists too: co-micronised glucosamine with curcumin restored locomotor function by 45% in an induced model, and a randomised double-blind curcuminoid study found significant pain reduction on manipulation in the treatment group and not in the control.

Format and demand

  • Soft chews are around 48% of product forms in the category, with a second source putting chewables at 43.84% for 2026. A chew is the dominant format, not a niche one.
  • Hip and joint is projected at 40.24% of the whole pet supplement market in 2026.
  • Around 35% of dogs over seven show signs of joint disorders, and about 55% of veterinary prescriptions for aging dogs include a joint health recommendation.
  • 39% of consumers prefer "veterinarian formulated" and 31% actively seek products described as clinically tested. That second figure is the commercial argument for the lab: an absorption study is exactly the evidence that buyer is looking for, and it is the thing a contract manufacturer competing on cost cannot put on the table.

No market size figure appears here deliberately. Two credible publishers are an order of magnitude apart on it, $139.79M against $1.1B, so neither is quotable and the structural percentages are used instead.

Open · regulatory and wording

Animal supplements sit under a different framework from human ones, the FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine and AAFCO rather than DSHEA. We are confirming the detail rather than guessing at it, and nothing here should be read as saying that framework is lighter.

The product language is joint health, mobility and comfort. Naming a disease would move the product from supplement toward drug, so that wording is settled before any label is printed rather than after.
