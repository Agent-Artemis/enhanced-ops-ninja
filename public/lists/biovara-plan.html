90-Day Plan
BioVara · white-label oral NAD

90-day implementation plan

Three 30-day blocks from 4 September to 2 December 2026. Every line has an owner and a week. Where an owner is not yet decided it says TBD, because an unassigned line is a decision to make rather than a gap to cover.

Who runs what

Jeff runs the venture, and personally works the health and wellness side.
Liposomal NAD, glutathione, vitamin C, and the veterinary product. Everything detailed in this plan sits here.
Ryan works the THC and CBD side, reporting into Jeff.
Ryan owns the work; Jeff owns the business result. That division is not documented here beyond its status, because Jeff does not work it.

How Jeff is paid for running it

$12,000 a month is the draw for running the business, not a retainer against selling. $2,000 a month covers the voice AI that books discovery calls. 25% of gross sales follows from running the whole venture, so it runs across both divisions rather than only the side Jeff works personally.

Profit share begins once revenue clears the threshold and replaces the draw rather than adding to it. Whether that is a hard switch or the greater of the two is an open decision below.

What month 3 is measured against
$48,000 / month

Gross sales at which the 25% covers the $12,000 draw. $56,000 a month covers the draw and the voice AI together. That is the goal line for the end of this plan and the point at which the draw stops being the way Jeff is paid.

Measured across both divisions. BioVara is currently running at roughly $80,000 to $90,000 a month between THC/CBD and peptides, so if the 25% applies across the business as described above, the crossover sits below existing run-rate rather than beyond it. That makes it a near-term threshold rather than a distant one, and it is the reason the scope question in Open Decisions matters more than it looks.

The one date that drives the plan

The NASC audit runs roughly four months, and it is the only item here whose lead time is longer than the plan itself. Started in week one, on 4 September, the seal lands around 4 January. This plan closes on 2 December.

So starting it in week one is not what opens the veterinary channel inside 90 days. The veterinary channel is a Q1 2027 revenue event, not a 90-day one. What can happen inside these 90 days is conversations: scoping the product with Page and Chris, and opening first contact with Mars, Banfield and NVA. What likely cannot happen without the seal is a supply agreement with a corporate group of that size.

Moving early is still worth paying for in Month 1, because it is what makes the channel openable in Q1 rather than Q2. Every other line in this plan can slip a fortnight without consequence; this one cannot move at all. One honest caveat we carry either way: we could not confirm that the channel formally requires the seal. If it turns out not to, the veterinary timeline improves.

The inputs this plan runs on

Who we call

42 researched target operators across nine segments, each from the company's own site. The five the plan opens with:

  • GameDay Men's Health, 400+ clinics. Already sells NAD+, and already sells an oral therapy, so their patients have proven they buy a take-home oral format.
  • Lindora, 35 clinics. Already sells NAD+ and already runs a Nutrition and Supplements category, so the retail shelf exists.
  • Forum Health. The only group publishing a partnerships inbox.
  • Restore Hyper Wellness, 210+ studios. Sells NAD on recurring plans.
  • Milan Laser, 400+ locations. Largest door count, corporate-owned, one decision reaches every location.

7 of the 42 carry named executives; the rest publish contact routes. Full list at enhancedops.ninja/lists/nad-targets.html.

How the calls open

The title we want mostly does not exist in this industry, so the first yes comes from a Chief Science Officer or CMO. The opening leads with formulation and data.

The lab is what a contract manufacturer cannot answer. An independent PhD-run lab, ex-Merck, working on the formulation: stability at delivery-truck temperatures, bioavailability across oral, sublingual, subcutaneous and IV, and an intracellular NAD assay with no market equivalent. That is third-party lab validation, and study data is available.

The pitch is oral, take-home, your label. The best-qualified chains already have an NAD ingredient partner, so the molecule conversation is occupied. The format is not.

The veterinary channel, and why NASC gates it

Pet NAD already exists at retail, on the same incumbent ingredient we meet on the human side. What we have not seen is pet NAD winning the veterinary clinic shelf, which is a different channel stocked through distributors and vet-exclusive brands. That is our reading of what we have found, not an established fact.

Distributors ship, so Covetrus, MWI Animal Health and Patterson replace our fulfilment rather than buy from us, and they are the route to independent clinics. Covetrus and MWI are merging, $1.25 billion in cash plus a 34.3% stake, taking three national distributors to two. The corporate groups are the white-label buyers: Mars Veterinary Health with 12,000+ veterinarians, Banfield with more than 1,000 hospitals and more than 3,600 veterinarians, and NVA with a network of 1,000 community hospitals and a named equine division. Two different conversations, and the plan does not blur them.

Regulatory is harder here than on the human side, not easier. DSHEA does not apply to animals, per FDA's Federal Register position of 22 April 1996: a product is food with claims tied to nutritive value, or the moment it makes a non-nutritive claim it is a drug. The category runs on FDA enforcement discretion, which is tolerance rather than legality. NASC is what makes it workable, and Preferred Supplier is the tier BioVara should hold: NASC Primary Suppliers can source from that tier without additional vendor qualification, which removes a due-diligence step for every prospective buyer. We could not confirm the channel formally requires the seal, but with a two-year third-party audit and a four-month lead time it should be treated as a precondition.

Risks

  • Page's financial position. If it worsens, supply is at risk and there is no second manufacturer in this plan. Worth knowing what the fallback is before it is needed rather than after.
  • The THC and CBD line's regulatory exposure. Jeff oversees that division and Ryan owns the work. A limit moving from 20mg to 5mg previously cost $100,000, so the exposure is demonstrated rather than theoretical.
  • Incumbent ingredient relationships at the best-qualified chains. Restore is on Niagen, The DRIPBaR on NR. This is why the pitch leads on format rather than on the molecule, but it still makes those two harder than their fit suggests.
  • NASC lead time gates the veterinary channel. Four months from a week-one start means early January. Any slip in starting it moves the whole channel by the same amount.
